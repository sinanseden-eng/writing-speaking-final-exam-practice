Master the art of engaging introductions, specific supporting examples, and structured, confident speech.

1. The Introduction

A great introduction doesn't just start the essay; it grabs the reader's attention and clearly states your opinion. Follow the H.B.T. Formula:

1. The Hook (Grab Attention)

Start with a question, a surprising fact, or an imaginative scenario.

"Imagine waking up to a world where plastic bags outnumber fish in the ocean."

2. The Bridge (Give Context)

Explain the background of the topic to connect your hook to the main idea.

"In recent years, environmental pollution has become one of the most serious challenges facing global communities."

3. The Thesis (Your Opinion)

Clearly state what your essay will prove.

"Because of this, single-use plastics must be completely banned to protect marine life and ensure a healthy future."

2. Supporting Examples

Vague body paragraphs lose points. When you make a point, you must back it up with a specific, real-world example.

Vague & Weak

"Technology makes learning easier because we can use things to study better."

Specific & Strong

"Technology makes learning easier. For instance, students can use VR headsets to visually explore historical sites like the Colosseum."

Target Language for Examples:

  • "A clear example of this is..."
  • "To illustrate this point, consider..."
  • "Take [Topic] for example. When..."
  • "Specifically, we can see this when..."

The Planning Pattern

How to organize a 1-Minute "Problem & Solution" speech effectively.

15s

State the Problem & Cause

Identify the issue clearly and say why it happens.

15s

Propose a Solution

Introduce your main idea to fix the issue.

20s

Explain How it Works (Detail)

Give a specific example of how this solution changes things.

10s

Conclusion

Summarize with a strong final thought.

Language Arsenal

The Problem
  • "One of the major issues is..."
  • "This problem primarily stems from..."
The Solution
  • "A highly effective solution would be..."
  • "To tackle this, we should..."

Model Answer Breakdown

Sample Prompt

"Traffic congestion is a major problem in many modern cities. What are some potential solutions to this?"

1. Problem & Cause (15s)

"One of the major issues in cities today is severe traffic congestion. This problem primarily stems from the fact that most people rely on personal cars to commute instead of public transport."

2. Propose Solution (15s)

"To tackle this, a highly effective solution would be for city governments to make all public transportation completely free."

3. Explain & Detail (20s)

"If buses and trains were free, thousands of commuters would leave their cars at home. Take Luxembourg, for example; when they made public transit free, it immediately reduced the number of cars on the road and lowered pollution."

4. Conclusion (10s)

"In short, removing fares for public transport is the most practical way to eliminate traffic jams and create a cleaner city."
